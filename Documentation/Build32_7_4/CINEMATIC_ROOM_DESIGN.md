# Cinematic Room Design

## Physical life

The upper/rear ventilation unit is the primary machine. Its housing, mounting legs, hub, guarded blade plane, fasteners, contact shadow, and green activity lamp make the motion physically legible. Company Server cooling is the secondary source. Both use visible workload/lifecycle presentation only; no result quality enters the mapping.

## Environmental life

Fixed profiles separate lighting, dust, cable, shadow, station, screen, network, and cooling phases. The central shaft evolves over 12.6 seconds; the fan shadow over 15.2; dust over 17.4; cable/paper airflow over 13.9/16.2. Seven motes remain inside the shaft and disappear when motion is reduced or the scene is inactive.

The room renderer remains mounted behind device focus. Command Focus therefore does not destroy ambient presentation state, and returning through LOOK OUT does not initialize every phase simultaneously.

## Materials and value

Walls combine diffuse concrete washes, staggered panels, seams, sparse scuffs, and nonuniform patches. The floor combines a diffuse perspective gradient, seams, aggregate, wear, and local screen reflections. Founder and station desks use matte laminate grain and restrained edge wear. Displays use controlled glass glare; stands/chassis use soft metal/plastic highlights; the server is predominantly powder-coated vented metal.

Peripheral zones rest darker than faces, equipment, and screens. Active visible work strengthens only the owning station’s local screen/desk/face spill; idle stations remain readable without equal illumination.

## Accessibility

Decorative fan, shadow, dust, texture, and reflection leaves are excluded from accessibility order. Increased Contrast strengthens the ventilation housing outline. Reduce Motion stops rotation, dust travel, shaft travel, cable/paper motion, and portrait displacement while retaining fan geometry, activity lamps, materials, hierarchy, and safe visible equipment state.
